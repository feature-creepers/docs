## Our mission

> **Pass on the love of building software.**

Code is one of the most fun things you can do with a computer, and the barrier to entry has never
been lower. We want more people to discover it, and to keep the fun alive for those who already did.

We do that in three ways:

<Cards>
  <Card icon={<span>🔨</span>} title="Build">
    Give everyone a playground to build what they want, serious or silly, alone or as a team.
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<span>🎓</span>} title="Learn & teach">
    Lower the barrier for newcomers with tutorials, pairing, and kind code reviews. Learn from each
    other, whatever your level.
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<span>📣</span>} title="Share">
    Ship in public, open source what we can, and write down what we learn so the next person has it
    easier.
  </Card>
</Cards>

## Our values

**Curiosity.** "How does this work?" and "what if we tried…?" are the two best sentences in software.
We follow them, even when they lead somewhere weird.

**Kindness.** We review code, not people. We assume good intent. We remember what it felt like to be
lost, because all of us were (and still are, regularly).

**Craft.** Dumb ideas deserve good code too. A useless feature that is well built, tested and
documented is a work of art.

**Playfulness.** If it is not fun, why are we doing it on a Saturday? Jokes in commit messages are
allowed. Easter eggs are encouraged.

**Openness.** We share our projects, our mistakes and our tutorials. Knowledge that stays in one head
is a bug.

## The Feature Creeper manifesto

Read it out loud. Preferably while deploying on a Friday. (Don't deploy on a Friday.)

<Steps>
<Step>

### Every idea deserves a branch

Dumb ideas are how good ideas get prototyped. Nobody here will ask "but why?" Well, they will, but out
of curiosity.

</Step>
<Step>

### "Useless" is a compliment

The most useless features are often the ones that teach you the most. Build the confetti cannon.

</Step>
<Step>

### Ship it, then laugh at it

Done and silly beats perfect and imaginary. A deployed joke teaches you more than a planned
masterpiece.

</Step>
<Step>

### Small commits, big dreams

Dream of 42 features. Ship them one commit at a time.

</Step>
<Step>

### There are no dumb questions, only undocumented answers

If you had to ask, someone else will too. Answer it once, then write it down.

</Step>
<Step>

### Review code, not people

Be honest about the code and gentle with the human. "This could be simpler" beats "this is wrong".

</Step>
<Step>

### Use the robots, keep the brain

AI coding agents are power tools. We use them a lot, and we stay the ones who understand, test and
own the code.

</Step>
<Step>

### Leave the codebase funnier than you found it

…but also cleaner. Both. Ideally at the same time.

</Step>
<Step>

### Finish things (sometimes)

Not every project needs to be finished. But when one is, we celebrate it loudly.

</Step>
<Step>

### One more feature. Always one more feature.

You knew this one was coming.

</Step>
</Steps>

## What we are not

- **Not a bootcamp.** We do not sell courses or certificates. Everything we teach is free and public.
- **Not a company.** Nobody is your boss here, and nobody will make you work on something you do not
  enjoy.
- **Not an elite club.** Your level does not matter. Your curiosity does.
